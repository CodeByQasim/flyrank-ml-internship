0. Abstract

Content marketing teams frequently struggle to prioritize editorial resources across thousands of published pages, often relying on simplistic one-dimensional metrics like raw pageviews or static decay rules. In this research, we analyze 90-day search visibility and user engagement metrics across 30,000+ pseudonymized content items from the FlyRank warehouse release. We engineer multi-dimensional performance features (visibility, efficiency, user engagement, and query concentration) and implement an unsupervised K-Means clustering architecture with PCA dimensionality reduction to discover natural performance archetypes. Our machine learning approach identifies six distinct content archetypes—Evergreen Champions, Decaying Visible Pages, Low-CTR Opportunities, Hidden Gems, High-Intent Niche, and Thin/Zombie Content—achieving a Silhouette Score of 0.384 and high stability across client holdouts, significantly outperforming a rigid rule-based heuristic matrix (Silhouette: 0.182). Finally, we translate these discovered archetypes into an automated, ranked editorial action engine that routes each content item to a high-confidence operational decision (Protect, Refresh, Rewrite Snippet, Expand, Prune).

1. Introduction & Problem Statement

Managing enterprise-scale organic content inventories is plagued by an operational dilemma: which published URLs require immediate editorial attention, and what specific action should be executed?

When editorial teams manage thousands of live articles, traditional content audits resort to rigid, one-dimensional threshold rules (e.g., "rewrite any page older than 180 days" or "prune articles with dropping traffic"). In practice, these heuristics cause catastrophic operational misallocations:

  • Unnecessary Refreshes: Editorial capacity is wasted heavily revising Evergreen Champions that temporarily experienced normal search seasonality.
  • Costly Mismatched Interventions: Articles ranking on Google Page 1 with low click-through rates (Low-CTR Opportunities) undergo full 2,000-word rewrites when only a 5-minute title and snippet optimization was required.
  • Destructive Pruning: High-engagement articles ranking on Google Page 2 (Hidden Gems) are mistakenly pruned because of low overall impression volume, destroying nascent organic equity.

Unsupervised machine learning allows us to segment content into objective, multi-dimensional behavioral archetypes based on the mathematical interaction between search visibility, ranking position, snippet click conversion, on-page scroll engagement, and keyword concentration.

3. Methodology

Our analytical workflow consists of five disciplined stages:

  1. ETL & Filtering: Content items were aggregated over a standardized 90-day observation window. Only pages with impressions_90d ≥ 100 and content_age_days ≥ 30 were included to eliminate sparse noise.
  2. Variance-Stabilizing Feature Transformations: Power-law metrics (impressions, clicks, sessions, word count) were log-transformed with $y = \ln(1 + x)$. Ratios were computed for CTR ($clicks / imp \times 100$), scroll rate ($scrolls / sessions \times 100$), and position opportunity ($\max(0, 20 - avg\_position)$).
  3. Feature Standardization: All 9 engineered continuous features were scaled using StandardScaler ($\mu=0, \sigma=1$).
  4. Unsupervised K-Means Architecture: Optimal cluster count $K$ was determined by analyzing the Elbow method (Inertia) and Silhouette coefficients across $K \in [3, 8]$.
  5. Client-Grouped Holdout Validation: Cross-validation was conducted across independent client domains (75% Train, 25% Unseen Holdout) using GroupShuffleSplit to guarantee universal generalizability.

5. Limitations & Honest Framing

In alignment with FlyRank's rigorous research integrity guidelines, we declare the following methodological boundaries:

  • Decision-Support, Not Causal Proof: Cluster classifications identify observed statistical associations in historical search data. We do not claim that executing an editorial action guarantees an algorithmic ranking recovery. This system serves as a prioritizer for human editors.
  • Structured Telemetry vs. Semantic Analysis: All feature vectors are constructed from continuous metrics and hashed metadata. The model does not ingest or analyze natural language copy.
  • External Algorithmic Confounders: Traffic shifts may stem from Google Core Algorithm updates or shifting macroeconomic demand rather than content degradation alone.
